      How we come up with the tags
      • Songs you upload, we listen to: tempo, time signature, mood and energy, whether there are drums, blues forms and, where the style model is installed, styles from 400 categories, moods and instruments. These count in full.
      • Songs from a link or a list, we look up by name on MusicBrainz, using the tags listeners gave the song or, more often, the artist. That describes the artist's music in general rather than this exact song, so each one counts for less (0.4 against 1). Upload the audio too and we'll listen to it as well.
      • A few limits: up to 100 songs, and we can't open private playlists. Lesser-known songs may not be on MusicBrainz yet, and the style model is surer about broad families than close neighbours. We show broad tags like “rock” but leave them out of the searches, because they lead straight back to the biggest names.

      Tick the instruments you can hear, or leave it all blank and we'll make our best guess.

      What ticking a box changes
      • Drums and bass decide whether you get a drum grid and a bass line. This matters most on phone recordings, where a bass guitar and a guitar's low strings sound alike.
      • One instrument on its own (say, solo guitar, piano or cello): we write its lowest notes out as their own part, in guitar notation for a guitar, with that instrument's range and sound in the MIDI file. A double bass or synth bass gets its own sound too.
      • Voice, or one lead instrument: we label the melody line as theirs.
      • What it can't do: apart from the drums and the lowest part, we don't write out each instrument separately. A guitar or piano in a band shows up in the chords and the melody line. Ticking it still helps: we know it's there, and so does the AI.

      Type the notes you hear and we'll analyse the melody, turn it into variations you can download as MIDI, and check each note against the recording.
